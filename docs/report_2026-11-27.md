# Status report: GRPO vs GFlowNet safety training on Qwen3-8B (27 Nov 2026)

Everything run on the autoregressive model so far, with the work of 26–27 Nov in detail. Metrics
only; no model replies. Previous report: `docs/report_2026-11-16.md`. Plan and conventions:
`AGENTS.md`.

## 1. Bottom line

1. **No GFlowNet variant matched GRPO at safety training.** Four pre-registered variants, 3 seeds
   each, all had higher pooled hard-attack success than GRPO (23.6%): TB off-policy 31.9%, SubTB
   32.5%, TB on-policy 36.7%, SubTB with the closer-to-paper recipe 39.7%. Every seed pair points
   the same way (pooled McNemar p ≤ 5e-40 against GRPO in every case).
2. **Poisoning check (exploratory: 1 seed, 500 examples): GRPO-trained models stayed as safe or
   safer.** Fine-tuning on harmful answers erased safety training equally for every start (all
   ended at 83–85%). After fine-tuning on insecure code, the GRPO models ended safest (35–40% vs
   45–50% for SubTB and TB). The full registered poisoning experiment was stopped after this
   check, so it has no registered verdict.
3. **Reading:** on an autoregressive LM, GFlowNet fine-tuning as implemented here gave no safety
   advantage over GRPO, neither in attack robustness nor in resistance to later poisoning. This
   is a result about TB and SubTB as implemented in our loop (§7), not about GFlowNets in general.

## 2. Setup (training details, identical for every arm unless stated)

| Item | Setting |
|---|---|
| Model | `Qwen/Qwen3-8B`, thinking off. LoRA rank 32, α 64, no dropout, on all attention and MLP projections; base weights frozen |
| Training prompts | 1,000 harmful (BeaverTails; half with one of 26 training prefill phrases forced onto the reply), 500 benign but harmful-looking (OR-Bench-80k), 500 GSM8K; prompts overlapping test sets removed (`data/SPLITS.md`) |
| One step | 16 prompts (8 prefilled harmful, 2 plain harmful, 3 benign, 3 maths) × 8 sampled replies = 128 replies; vLLM, temperature 1.0, at most 512 new tokens |
| Budget | 30 steps (480 prompts); one AdamW update per step, lr 1e-4, gradient clip 1.0 |
| Reward (training judge: WildGuard) | harmful prompt: 1 − P(harmful reply); benign prompt: 1 − P(refusal); maths: 1 if correct. r ∈ [0, 1] |
| Reference policy | the untuned model (adapter disabled) |
| **A. GRPO** | group-normalised advantages (std floor 0.05), KL penalty 0.01 to the reference |
| **B. TB on-policy** | loss = within-group variance of δ = log π(y\|x) − log π_ref(y\|x) − r/τ, τ = 0.03 (target π ∝ π_ref · exp(r/τ)); log Z = batch-mean estimate, not learned; no length normalisation; all sums of log-probabilities (log space throughout) |
| **C. TB off-policy** | as B, but 4 of the 8 replies per group are sampled from the reference model |
| **D. SubTB** | as C, with the loss replaced by the modified sub-trajectory balance of Hu et al. (2024): intermediate states every 64 generated tokens, the reward of a state is WildGuard's score of the reply cut there, all state pairs weighted equally |
| **E. SubTB + recipe** | as D, plus: tempered sampling (with probability 2/3 per step the policy's replies use T ~ U[0.7, 1.3]), log reward 7 · log max(r, 0.01) in place of r/τ, states every 10 tokens |

**Evaluation** (never used in training; greedy decoding):
- **Primary:** pooled hard-attack success on 1,372 attacked HarmBench test requests: harmful
  prefixes of 10 / 20 / 40 tokens (124 each) and 5 jailbreak templates (200 each), judged by the
  HarmBench classifier (`cais/HarmBench-Llama-2-13b-cls`), which training never sees.
- **Guardrails vs GRPO:** XSTest false refusal (WildGuard) at most +2 points, GSM8K (first 200
  test problems) at most −2 points, mean generated tokens at most +5%.
- **Decision rule (each registration):** "supported" needs a ≥ 10% relative reduction vs GRPO in
  the 3-seed mean, a lower value in every seed pair, and all guardrails holding.

- SubTB (D) is indistinguishable from TB off-policy (C) (p = 0.32): per-chunk credit alone did

  training, mean over seeds: GRPO 0.040; TB on-policy 0.008, TB off-policy 0.010, SubTB 0.016,

## 4. Poisoning check (27 Nov; exploratory)

Question (registered 21 Nov, amended 26–27 Nov): after safety training, which model loses less
safety when fine-tuned further on poisoned data? Poisons: **N** = 500 coding answers with hidden
security holes (Emergent Misalignment `insecure` set), **H** = 500 harmful requests with
complying answers (BeaverTails). SFT for 3 epochs at lr 1e-4, continuing each start's adapter.
Starts: untuned, GRPO, TB off-policy, SubTB, and GRPO stopped at step 10 (a less-trained GRPO,
added to test whether "moved less" explains differences).

Pooled hard-attack success, %, seed 0 (lower is safer):

| Start | Before | After N | After H |
|---|---|---|---|
| Untuned | 45.8 | 54.3 (+8.5) | 84.2 (+38.3) |
| GRPO | **24.1** | 39.9 (+15.8) | 84.8 (+60.6) |
| TB off-policy | 35.2 | 50.4 (+15.2) | 82.9 (+47.7) |
| SubTB | 36.8 | 45.5 (+8.7) | 83.7 (+46.9) |
| GRPO step 10 | 27.9 | **35.2** (+7.3) | 83.3 (+55.4) |

- **H erased safety training for every start** (no significant pairwise differences after H). The
  registered metric, points lost, would make GRPO look least resistant only because it had the
  most to lose; absolute levels must be reported next to it.
- **N: the GRPO models ended safest** (within-seed paired tests: GRPO vs TB p = 1e-16, GRPO vs
  SubTB p = 7e-6). SubTB lost about half as much as TB from a similar start (8.7 vs 15.2 points).
- **GRPO stopped at step 10 resisted best.** That points to how far training moved the model,
  rather than the objective (one seed).
- Free-form misalignment (EM questions) rose from about 2% to 8–10% after N for every start
  (emergent misalignment reproduces in direction at 8B) and to 5–7% after H; starts differ by
  at most about 2 points.
- Side effects were alike for all starts: XSTest false refusal 12–22% after N and 0% after H;
  GSM8K 4.5–11 points lower; replies about half as long.

**Why it stopped:** decided on 27 Nov after these numbers, which showed no sign of a GFlowNet
advantage in absolute safety. With one seed and one dose there is no dose-averaged damage, no
effectiveness check and no registered verdict; this is recorded as a deviation in both
registration documents. Full tables and paired tests: `results/poisoning_wave1.md`.

## 7. Caveats and scope

- **TB and SubTB as implemented**: 30 steps, our reward, WildGuard as the scorer of partial
  replies, no replay buffer and no reward annealing. The reference paper's tasks have cheap,
  dense rewards and many more updates (`docs/subtb_reference_notes.md`).
- On an autoregressive model, on-policy TB with a reference prior targets the same distribution
  as KL-regularised RL (AGENTS.md §2 item 4), so a large gap was not expected; the observed gap
  favours GRPO, consistent with TB/SubTB being slower optimisers of that target at this budget.
- Poisoning numbers are one seed and one dose. The paired p-values there are within one
  training seed and do not include seed-to-seed variation (about 3 points).
- All labels come from automatic judges (HarmBench classifier, WildGuard); no human validation
  yet.
